# Hola

Hola is Spanish for Norwegians, ten minutes a day, at https://hola.ymse.land. It is Tian's sibling (tian.ymse.land, Mandarin) and one of the sites of Ymseland. The Spanish is Spain's (ordenador, zumo, vosotros), and everything else the page says is Norwegian bokmål: each page's title is in Spanish with the Norwegian under it. The Norwegian is plain and practical, as a good teacher speaks: the user found the literary manner (Cervantes' chapter headings, «Hvori leseren …») a distracting gimmick, and the aim is to learn good, useful Spanish.

## The repo

- Astro with one React island (`client:only`), Tailwind v4 and lucide-react, built to static files. The app is `src/app/`; paths below without a folder are in it. `src/pages/index.astro` holds it, `om.astro` says what Hola is and what it keeps, and `404.astro` is the page for a wrong address.
- The word bank and the settings live in the browser's localStorage (`hola:bank:v1`, `lib/store.tsx`), and a backup file under Innstillinger moves them to another browser (banks are merged, each link keeping its latest answer).
- An account is optional: anyone can sign in with Google, and Convex (project `hola`) then keeps the bank on the account too (`convex/bank.ts`, as the page's own JSON), merged with the browser's the same way on sign-in and saved two seconds after a change (`lib/account.tsx`). `convex/access.ts` keeps who has signed in, which `/admin` lists for the admin. Each new account is sent to the admin's inbox at admin.ymse.land, with the sender's token in `YMSELAND_NOTIFY_TOKEN` on production's Convex (without it, nothing is sent). A build takes production's address from `.env.production` (committed; public, like every Convex URL), and `npm run dev` the development deployment's from `.env.local`. Without `PUBLIC_CONVEX_URL` (`lib/cloud.tsx`) the page shows no sign-in and asks nothing of Convex.
- Hosting: the Cloudflare Worker `hola`, built by Workers Builds on a push to `main`, on the custom domain hola.ymse.land. The files are static assets; `worker/index.ts` runs only for `/api/auth/*`, passing Google's sign-in on to production's Convex site (`CONVEX_SITE` in `wrangler.jsonc`; empty, it would pass nothing on), so that Google's page names hola.ymse.land (Convex's `CUSTOM_AUTH_SITE_URL`). Headers are in `public/_headers`. Pages build to `om.html`, so addresses have no trailing slash.
- Changes in `convex/` go to production with the site: the deploy command is `npx convex deploy && npx wrangler deploy`, with a deploy key as the build secret `CONVEX_DEPLOY_KEY`. The backend goes first, so when removing a function, push the frontend that stops calling it in an earlier push.
- Google sign-in has a Cloud project, client and brand of its own: project «Hola» (`hola-512421`), client «Hola», with `https://hola.ymse.land/api/auth/callback/google` as its only redirect URI, so Google's page says «Hola». The brand is verified and the app published (External), with `/om` as its home page, `/personvern` as its privacy policy and `ymse.land` as its authorized domain; keep `/personvern` in step with `convex/schema.ts`. Changing the app's name, home page or privacy link means verifying the brand again, and a logo means a full app review. Production's Convex is `careful-perch-235` (eu-west-1), with `SITE_URL` and `CUSTOM_AUTH_SITE_URL` https://hola.ymse.land; development's has no Google sign-in.

Before committing, run `npm run lint && npm test && npm run format:check && npm run build && npm run test:e2e`. Commit as you go. ## The app

`index.tsx` is the shell: one page per hash (`/#palabra`, `/#quijote/molinos`), each loaded lazily from `games/` and listed in `registry.tsx`, and the home page (`home.tsx`) without one. The home page is in four parts, one shown at a time, picked from a bar at the foot of a phone's screen or a row under the picture: I dag (today's chapter, the story to go on with, the credential), Les (the stories), Spill (the games in three groups, `GAME_GROUPS`) and Ordene (the word count and Tu museo); the part last shown is kept for the visit. `mend.tsx` catches what breaks, so the page never goes blank: a page's code gone after a deploy reloads the page once. `daily.tsx` lets a game opened from today's chapter lead back to it. The page installs as an app (`public/manifest.webmanifest`, icons in `public/app/`, drawn from `icon.svg` by `node scripts/icons.mjs`).

- **The questions** (`quiz.tsx`): a word to understand is asked written or, now and then, said (`BY_EAR`), and the day's listening asks every word by ear. A word heard is said once, as its question opens, and again only when asked for (the user found it said twice in a row); an answer, right or wrong, stays until Neste is pressed, so that what it says can be read and its word tapped (the user's wish).
- **The word bank** (`lib/bank.ts`): a word is two links, learnt apart, input and output (the user's choice, since Spanish is written as it sounds): `leer`, Entender on the page (understanding it, read or heard), and `decir`, Recordar (coming up with the Spanish from its meaning, with the article). The ids are the links' first names, kept so that saved banks still read; hearing was once a link of its own, `oir`, and `merge` reads a card saved with it as the better of the two. Each has a memory in the manner of FSRS, and every game records its answers with a weight for how much they say: typing a word says more than picking it. Games ask about the link that has faded most.
- **Words** (`data/words.ts`): lesson by lesson, one line per word, in the order they are met. `isClue` says whether a word can be a clue in the crossword, the ring, the word search, the soft clock and the chain (not greetings, phrases or articles); in today's chapter those games wait until enough clue words are met (`NEEDS` in `lib/today.ts`).
- **The starting point** (`data/start.ts`): Hola is made for someone who knows elementary Spanish, so every bank starts with the simplest words known and many easy ones understood (read without the Norwegian) but still practised for coming up with them (`withStart` in `lib/bank.ts`), as cards marked as never answered, which give way to real answers in a merge, and which neither hold today's new words back when they fade nor count among the words fading tomorrow in the day's summary; a review asks such a card about either link, at random, since its memories were only guessed at. Raise `START` when a list grows.
- **Themes** (`data/themes.ts`, `games/tema.tsx`): the lessons beyond the first ones, in groups on the home page, each with a few lines on what it is about. Any number can be chosen, each with a priority from 1 to 3 (Av, 1, 2, 3 on the home page or on a theme's page; `bank.theme.on`, the user's wish, as in Tian), and today's chapter then meets their words before the rest, in rounds of as many from each as its priority (`toMeet`), from today's new words on if none of them is begun (`retheme`). A bank saved before priorities (`{ ids, t }`) or when one theme could be chosen (`{ id, t }`) is read as those at 1. A bank starts with the everyday themes (`THEMES_AT_START` in `data/start.ts`: the bar and the shop at 2, getting about, the weather, the hours and directions at 1), given by `withStart` only while its themes have never been chosen (`t` 0), so any choice since, even of none, is kept and wins in a merge.
- **Today's chapter** (`lib/today.ts`, `games/hoy.tsx`), called Dagens økt on the page: made once a day and kept in the bank, so it can be taken up where it was left: the words fading, a few new ones, a game or a chapter of a story, and a little listening. Each day finished is a stage of the Camino to Santiago and a stamp in the credential (`data/camino.ts`, `games/credencial.tsx`).
- **The stories** (`data/stories.ts`, read in `games/story.tsx`): Don Quijote first (`data/quijote.ts`), the start of the novel retold simply, in mixed text (`lib/text.ts`): every word is in Spanish, with the Norwegian over it until it is known (brown while not met, blue while practised), and alone once known; Norwegian is never mixed into the Spanish. The markup is described at the top of `data/quijote.ts`, and the tests check that every word is in the bank. Each story is a page of its own in `registry.tsx`, all with the same reader; a chapter's id is unique across the stories, since the bank keeps the chapters read by id, and today's chapter takes the next one unread, story by story. A chapter may have a painted picture (`img`) where Don Quijote has its ink drawings. The home page's index (`INDEX` in `home.tsx`) shows a story not written yet faded.
- **A word's card** (`PeekCard` in `ui.tsx`): a word tapped in mixed text, anywhere, opens it, and so does a word shown once it is answered or found, in a question's answer and the games' lists of words (`Es` with `peek`; never where the word is the question): the word as the bank has it, its meaning, a noun's gender, and where the page passes them (`explain` on `Mixed`) what this form is and a note on it here. **Kan det** makes it known (the Norwegian over it goes, and it comes back in practice now and then); **Øv på det** meets it.
- **The library** (`data/biblioteca.ts`, `data/libros/`, read in `games/biblioteca.tsx`; Biblioteca under Les): texts on shelves (`SHELVES`), each under a heading of its own: first five folk tales from E. S. Harrison's _An Elementary Spanish Reader_ (1912, public domain on Project Gutenberg), as they were written, not retold, with only the spelling brought up to date; then texts written for Hola, a text to a chapter: La dama del abanico azul (`libros/dama*.ts`), a crime story in seven chapters set in Toledo, for the user's mother, who likes crime of every kind, murder and mystery included. Hola's own texts are in present tense and short sentences, and a chapter's `about` does not give its turn away. The whole text is in mixed text with room between the lines, and «Aa» opens the choices, each kept in the settings: the text's size (`textSize`) and width (`textWidth`), scrolling or pages turned sideways as in Kulturuke's reader (`paged`; CSS columns as tall as the screen, turned by a swipe, the arrow keys or a tap in the margin, with no buttons under the page, the user's choice; the default, with the narrow width and the second size), the title and «Aa» as wide as the text and hidden once reading goes on (a page turned, a scroll down), back on a tap on the page or a scroll up, as in Kulturuke, and the Norwegian over the words (`gloss`): none («Bare spansk», the lines then close as in a book), over the words not known yet, or over all of them, faint over the known. No reading aloud and no music (the user's choice: it is for reading; the guitar, if on, is quiet while a text is open, `MusicKeeper` in `index.tsx`), and the place read to kept in the bank (`bank.place`: the paragraph and the word in it, the later of two in a merge), so the page opens there again. The lines are in the stories' markup, plus `#key` for a note in the text's `notes`, and each text has `forms` saying what its verb forms are. The words a text needs beyond the bank are a lesson of its own in `data/words.ts`, marked `book`: met only from the reader, never among today's new words (`TAUGHT`). «Ferdig lest» counts the words met and not looked up as read. Keep an old text's Spanish as it was written; `tests/biblioteca.test.ts` checks that every word is in the bank and every word of its lesson is used.
- **Reading aloud** (`lib/aloud.ts`, used in `games/story.tsx`): «Les høyt» reads the whole chapter in Narrador's voice, a line at a time with a short pause after each, marking the line read and keeping it in view. Pause, Fortsett and Stopp control it, and a word looked up pauses it.
- **The mystery** (`data/caso.ts`, `lib/caso.ts`, `games/caso.tsx`): El caso del reloj perdido, in Cadaqués, is a story told by ear (`listen`): each line is said as it comes, in the voice of whoever says it (`who`), and shows as its sound wave until its text is asked for or the questions are answered. The words of a line only heard count too, when two answers of three are right. The cast's voices are in `data/voices.ts`: Narcís, Remei and Laia designed, from Cadaqués, the others prebuilt, and the inspector Narrador's. Each chapter writes a clue in the inspector's notebook (`clue`), shown on the story's page, and the last stops to ask who took the watch (`accuse`): a wrong guess gets a hint back to the chapter that rules it out.
- **Speech** (`lib/speech.ts`): Spanish recorded ahead with Gemini TTS on the user's Mac (see The recordings, below), played in one audio element (Safari lets an element play by itself only once a touch has started it, so the first touch wakes it and a word a game says by itself is heard too), at Ajustes' speed and slower for the snail, between half and twice the normal speed. A text without a recording, or one that will not play, is said by the browser's own Spanish voice, Spain's first; Ajustes offers the browser's voices only while there are no recordings. One thing is said at a time, and `?mute` silences the page for tests: speech still takes its time but is not heard. The two voices are designed from a description in `data/voices.ts`, both from Madrid with distinción: Profesora says the words, their forms in the story (a tapped word's card), the plurals and adjective forms Letras takes (`SAID` in `lib/letras.ts`) and the sample in Ajustes; Narrador says the story's lines (in the mystery, the narration and the inspector's) and the games' sentences. `lib/spoken.ts` lists everything said aloud, so anything new the page says must be listed there. Sounds (`lib/sound.ts`) and the guitar (`lib/music.ts`) are made with Web Audio, with no files.
- **The bar and the market** (`lib/tapas.ts`, `lib/rastro.ts`): Tapas' four guests (Lucía, Manolo, Carmen, Javi) and El Rastro's four sellers (Paco, Rosa, Julián, Pilar) speak in Gemini's prebuilt voices, each with a manner of its own in `data/voices.ts`; the player's own lines at the market are Profesora's. What they say is a fixed list (`tapasLines`, `rastroLines`), so that each line can have a recording, and Profesora says each form in it for a tapped word's card. A seller's line is written out only once it is answered or looked at, and only a decisive answer to a price heard and not read is recorded.
- **Tu museo** (`lib/colecciones.ts`, `games/museo.tsx`, shown on the home page's Ordene under Tus palabras, the user's wish): a reward to look at, with nothing to answer. A lesson is begun once a word of it is met, and finished once every word is met and four in five are learnt (reading lasting a day and a half or more, however long ago). A lesson once finished stays so: the store passes every change to the bank through `keep`, which notes each lesson found finished in `bank.kept`, so nothing is taken back, whether the player is away or slips on a word. The museum has a room for each group of themes; a theme's picture (`data/museo.ts`, a print or a poster) is hung once it is finished, shown as a drawn stand-in (`games/museoArt.tsx`) until it is painted as `museo/<lesson>`, from the prompts in `data/museo-prompts.ts`. (Los cajones, a chest of drawers with a keepsake for each lesson, was removed at the user's wish.)
- **Sounds and stress** (`data/sonidos.ts`, `lib/sonidos.ts`, `lib/acento.ts`): Sonidos' chart and its sets of words that sound alike are made of Hola's own words, some from a lesson of their own (Letras y sonidos); the nouns it says without their article are `SAID` in `lib/sonidos.ts`, in Profesora's voice. Only its ear records; the dictation and Acento record nothing. `syllables` and `stressIndex` in `lib/spanish.ts` divide a word as the RAE does and find its stress, for Cadena and Acento alike.
- **The numbers and the taxi** (`lib/numeros.ts`, `lib/taxi.ts`): Números' sentences are a fixed deck of 250 from a seeded generator, said in turn by Lola and Andrés (the two designed voices, each with a style of its own), and the Christmas lottery is sung by Ildefonso, Gemini's prebuilt Leda in a child's sing-song. Taxi's passenger, Pasajero, says a fixed set of lines (`TAXI_LINES` in `data/taxi.ts`), politely with usted, and Profesora says each form in them for a tapped word's card. The town is a plan seen from straight above, a grid of five streets each way with a garden belt round it (`games/taxiMap.tsx` draws it flat until the painting `taxi/map` is made, then only its streets, faintly, over the painting), and the game puts its own signs on the places. The map turns under the taxi, which always points up the screen, so that left and right are the player's own; four big buttons steer (left, straight on, right, stop), and a move that cannot be made where the taxi is is greyed out (`movesAt`). An instruction followed is recorded, with more weight when heard than when its words were shown. A ride is planned to ask more of the ear than izquierda and derecha, which the buttons show (`demands` in `lib/taxi.ts`): a turn after a crossing or two straight on, told by its count or by a place on the corner (never the one the ride sets out from), and, nearly always, another place on the right to drive past before the one to stop at, a way two crossings longer if need be; the places stand together in three blocks for that. Each wrong move costs a star, and the passenger's stars dim under their portrait as it happens.

## The recordings

They are made on the user's Mac, where the Gemini key is (`GEMINI_API_KEY` from the environment, `.env.local` or the Keychain, kept with `security add-generic-password -a "$USER" -s GEMINI_API_KEY -w`), with uv and ffmpeg. Nobody can call Gemini from elsewhere, so a change that needs new recordings is finished there:

```bash
mkdir -p .cache && node scripts/speech.ts > .cache/speech.json
uv run scripts/audio.py .cache/speech.json --dry-run --job   # what would be asked for
uv run scripts/audio.py .cache/speech.json --job             # make it
```

`scripts/audio.py` asks for each item on its own. With `--job` it uses Gemini's batch mode, at half the price, answered within a day and often within minutes; the jobs are noted in `.cache/jobs.json`, so a run cut short picks them up again. Without it, up to `--max` (10) are asked for directly, or those named with `--only "text"`, to try a voice; `--voice` keeps to one voice's items. Each answer is trimmed of its silence (the quiet edges, such as a final s, are kept) and becomes `public/audio/<id>.mp3`, named by a hash of the file itself, and `data/audio.json` maps `voice|text` to it; files no longer used are removed. `public/_headers` lets browsers keep `/audio/*` for a year as immutable, which is safe because a recording made again gets a new name. The designed voices' ids are kept in `scripts/voices.json`: delete one to design that voice again, then `--remake <name>` (or `--remake words`, `lines`) to read its items again. At the end it names the recordings far from the length of their text, and words said with a pause inside them (la ... dama), to listen to or make again. Commit `public/audio/`, `data/audio.json` and `scripts/voices.json`. Once `audio.json` has entries, `tests/speech.test.ts` fails while anything listed has no recording, or a file is left over or not named by its hash.

## The audiobooks

`data/lydbok.ts` holds audiobooks of fifteen to twenty minutes, each written for one reader's words, made as a podcast with two hosts (the user's choice): Marta, from Madrid and long in Oslo, and Ingrid, a Norwegian of the listener's age learning Spanish, both designed voices that talk in Norwegian and Spanish within a line, as Tian's hosts do. They give a scene's new words, the scene's lines are read in Spanish by the narrator and the cast (the stories' markup, so its words are in the bank), and they pick out phrases after it. `games/lydbok.tsx` plays it as one file, with its chapters to jump to; the text, in mixed text with the line heard marked and each line tapped heard from where it begins, shows only when asked for («Les med», kept in the browser), since one may want to listen without reading. `/#lydbok` lists them and the home page's Les shows them. They are a podcast too, to follow in an app (Pocket Casts and others): the feed `/podcast.xml` and each book's chapters (`/podcast/<file>.json`) are written at build time (`lib/podcast.ts`, `src/pages/podcast*`), so a book pushed with its `date` is a new episode, and the feed, like Tian's, asks not to be listed in directories. The cover is `public/podcast/cover.jpg`, drawn from the icon by `node scripts/icons.mjs`. `tests/lydbok.test.ts` checks the story's words, the length and, for a book written from the starting bank, at most eight new words a chapter. A book is written from a reader's bank: with the reader's wish, `npx convex run --prod bank:forEmail '{"email": "…"}' > .cache/bank.json` on the Mac (an internal query) and `node scripts/lydbok-ord.ts .cache/bank.json` list the words known and practised, the weakest first, to write from. Once its lines are recorded (The recordings, above), it is put together on the Mac:

```bash
mkdir -p .cache && node scripts/lydbok.ts > .cache/lydbok.json
uv run scripts/lydbok.py .cache/lydbok.json
```

which writes `public/lydbok/<id>-<hash>.mp3` and `data/lydbok-times.json` (when each line begins). Commit both.

## The pictures

Painted pictures are made on the user's Mac too, with the image tool of the Codex CLI on the user's ChatGPT plan (signed in with `codex login`) and ImageMagick. `scripts/pictures.mjs` holds each picture's description. Every painting is a travel poster of the 1920s–30s (`POSTER` in `data/museo-prompts.ts`, with `design/sketches/reiseplakat.jpg` as the reference for the style), the style the user chose for Hola throughout, the home page and Don Quijote included, from the round Hola-stiler in moodboard:

```bash
node scripts/pictures.mjs --list          # what is painted, and what is not
node scripts/pictures.mjs stories/caso    # paint those missing whose names begin so (none named: all)
node scripts/pictures.mjs --mouths        # the speakers' open mouths, as edits of their waiting faces
node scripts/pictures.mjs --web           # cut them to shape into public/ and list them
```

Each takes a minute or two and counts against the plan's limits. The full-size paintings stay in `.cache/pictures/`; delete one there to paint it again. `--web` saves each as `public/<name>.webp`, cut to the shape it is shown in, and lists the names in `data/pictures.ts`, so that a page shows a picture only once it is made, and its drawing until then: a chapter of a story takes `stories/<story>/<chapter id>`, and a picture in Tu museo `museo/<lesson>`. The home page's scene is La Mancha painted four times, `home/day`, `home/dawn`, `home/dusk` and `home/night` (`data/home-prompts.ts`), the same landscape in another light: day is painted first, and the other three take it as a second reference, so paint `home/day` and look at it before the rest. The home page's small squares, the story index's `toc/<story>` and the game tiles' `games/<game>` (`data/icon-prompts.ts`, names in `data/icons.ts`), are one bold subject each, painted square as a matching set and fitted whole into the ink drawing's box (`TocArt`, `GameIcon` in `art.tsx`). The Camino's stamps (`camino/<town>`, `data/camino-prompts.ts`, the user's wish) are posters made into stamps: each town's own subject in flat poster colours inside a border in one ink, looking pressed on the paper, painted as cut-outs and set in the middle of their square (`gravity`); `camino/roncesvalles` is painted first and the rest take it as a second reference, so paint it and look at it before them. Until a stamp is painted, `Stamp` in `art.tsx` draws it in ink. Games that have people in them (El Rastro, Tapas, El caso, Palabras, Números) get a painted background and cut-out figures that breathe and change face as the game goes: a picture marked `cutout` is painted on a transparent background and trimmed, and one with `parts` is a sheet of four busts of the same person, cut apart into `<name>-<face>` at the same size and baseline. The busts of a sheet never quite match (the head turned, set higher), so the talking face is not the sheet's: `--mouths` paints the waiting face again as an edit with only the mouth open (as Puppet Theater does), and `--web` then makes `<name>-talk` just that mouth, clear around it (`scripts/mouths.py`), which the games show over the waiting face (never over another), so that only the mouth moves when they speak, never the head, and not even the grain. Each game's prompts are in `data/<game>-prompts.ts`, read only by `scripts/pictures.mjs`, and the numbers page and prompt share (the faces, where a table or counter edge lies) in `data/<game>Layout.ts`; measure such an edge on the picture as `--web` cuts it, not on the full painting. Taxi's town, `taxi/map`, is painted from the plan the game draws itself, `design/sketches/taxi-layout.png` (made by `node scripts/taxi-layout.mjs`; make it again when the town changes), so that its streets land where the game's are. Give each picture a subject of its own, so that the museum does not repeat a story's chapter. Look at each picture before committing it, and paint again what is off. Commit the `.webp` files and `data/pictures.ts`.

## The look

The home page and the pages around the games are Don Quijote in ink on paper, with a wash of colour (`hola.css`, classes `q-`; the drawings are in `art.tsx`, roughened by the filters in `ui.tsx`). The user has asked for the travel-poster style throughout, so the ink drawings give way to paintings as they are made: Don Quijote's chapters, and the picture at the top of the home page, which, once all four paintings are made, is La Mancha as a travel poster that follows the player's clock (`sky.tsx`, with the time of day and the moon in `lib/sky.ts`): the paintings crossfade around sunrise and sunset, and the moon in its real phase, the stars, the clouds and the birds are drawn over them in the same flat colours, still with reduced motion. `?time=21:30` (or `?date=2027-02-06&time=20:00`) shows another hour; until the paintings are made, the ink drawing stays. Each game has a look of its own, with its own fonts (`useFont`) and a CSS file whose classes have a prefix of their own (`g5-`, `d4-`, `r6-`, `c6-`, …), and takes its ground and colours from its painted tile (`games/<game>`), in the posters' flat colours (sea green, mustard, terracotta, slate blue, cream, deep blue) on grained paper, never gloss or glow. They follow the user's taste in flat graphic printmaking and posters (woodcut, linocut, hand-coloured woodcut, paper-cut, ligne claire, 1930s travel posters) in Spanish forms such as azulejos, aleluyas, the baraja española and feria posters, after the sketches in `design/sketches/`, painted with ChatGPT, which the user allows freely. The user dislikes glossy 3D, photographs, neon, painterly oil, crowds and kitsch. Dalí's works are protected until the end of 2059, so draw anew and never copy a painting, and leave out the Chupa Chups logo. No emoji in the graphics; lucide icons for plain controls.

Astro puts the games' CSS before `hola.css`, so a rule in `hola.css` beats a game's rule of the same weight. Keep the shell's rules on elements weak (`:where(…)`), and never style bare elements there.

## Privacy

Without an account nothing leaves the browser. Signed in, the account keeps the name and address Google gives, and the word bank, in Convex in the EU (Ireland); the sign-in's own cookies last only while it is under way. No analytics and no tracking. The fonts come from Google Fonts. `/om` and `/personvern` say so; keep them in step with what is stored.
